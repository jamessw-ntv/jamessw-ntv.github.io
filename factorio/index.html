Factorio — Build Guide

One thing at a time: the guide shows what to research or build next, exactly where, and how big. Tick it off and the next one appears. Sync from your save to fill in your resources and tick what's already built.

Raw resources

Every recipe line

Every block on the map is built from one of these line patterns, so a block's footprint is exact for the pattern: its width is machines × pitch and its height is the line height. One square = one tile.

How to use this

  1. Pick a layout. Your world places every block around your real resource patches. The main bus is the classic first megabase: one wide trunk of belts with factories peeling off it. City blocks are a grid of 100×100 squares ringed by rail, one product per square.
  2. Set your science per minute (SPM) and module setup. Every count follows these.
  3. Open ✅ Next step. It shows one thing to do. Do it in game, press Done, and the next one appears. Ticks are saved in this browser.
  4. Press Sync from my game whenever you like. It reads your resources, what you've built and what you've researched, then ticks finished steps for you.

Syncing from your save

Factorio's save files can't be read outside the game, so the planner gives you a one-line console command instead. It writes a small file, factorio-planner.json, to the game's script-output folder with three things: where iron, copper, stone, coal, oil and water are (within 800 tiles of your character), every assembler, furnace, chemical plant, refinery, silo and lab with its recipe and position, the list of finished research, your map tags, and enemy bases near your base. Upload that file and the planner places your patches, avoids your existing buildings, keeps blocks you've already built where they are, keeps 40 tiles clear of biter nests, and ticks what's done.

On Chrome or Edge you can skip the upload: press Connect my script-output folder once and pick that folder. After that, run the command in game, switch back to this tab and it refreshes by itself, or press ⟳ Refresh from game. Other browsers use the upload button.

Commands starting with /c turn off achievements for that save. Run it on a copy of the save if you care about them.

Your world

Drag each resource patch to roughly where it sits on your map (one square of the faint grid is a 32×32-tile chunk). The planner then places every block, smelters first, as close as it can to the rate-weighted middle of whatever feeds it, without overlapping a patch or another block. Move a patch and everything re-plans live; the "belt-tiles" figure shows how much hauling the layout needs, so you can compare spots. Add extra patches if you have several of one kind; each block draws from the nearest.

Mark landmarks (a lake, spawn, a choke point) and biter nests on the map panel; map tags and enemy bases come in by themselves when you sync. Blocks stay 40 tiles clear of a nest, and the dashed red ring is the gap to wall and turret. Drag your own marks to move them.

The step slider above the map replays the plan: drag it to any guide step to see what's built by then (solid, ✓), what that step builds (green), and what comes later (faint, its space kept free). It follows your progress until you move it.

What "endgame" means here

The base game's finish line on Nauvis: all seven science packs, including space science from rocket launches, running continuously for infinite research. Each rocket is 100 rocket parts plus a satellite, and returns 1,000 space science packs.

Assumptions

  • Recipes, machine speeds, module effects and the beacon falloff curve are copied from Factorio's own data files (base mod 2.1.21, wube/factorio-data), not from memory.
  • Belts are express (blue), 45 items/s. Mining drills are electric drills (0.5 ore/s, +10% per mining productivity level). Pumpjacks are counted at 100% yield (10 crude/s), plus mining productivity.
  • Oil uses advanced oil processing, cracking heavy into light and light into petroleum only as much as needed. Rocket fuel and solid fuel are made in the oil block from light oil.
  • Beacons follow the 2.x rule: each extra beacon adds less (the effect is divided by the square root of the beacon count). 8 beacons with two Speed 3 each give +424% speed.
  • Labs: counted for a 60-second research unit with +250% lab research speed. Labs and the rocket launch animation aren't on the critical path for counts.
  • Power is an estimate: machines and labs at full load, plus beacons (about five per machine in the beaconed row pattern) and mining drills. Inserters, idle drain, and the silo's extra draw while launching are left out.
  • Science per minute is an upper bound: no infinite technology uses all seven packs at once. Rocket launches take time too, so at 1,000 SPM and above a second silo keeps up more easily.
  • Pumpjacks count mining productivity, as drills do. Until Advanced oil processing your refineries run basic processing (petroleum only), so the oil block is sized for the advanced recipe it ends up with.

Space Age

This plan is the base game. With the Space Age expansion the rocket needs 50 parts, space science is made on a space platform instead of from satellites, and the endgame moves to other planets, so it would be a different plan.
